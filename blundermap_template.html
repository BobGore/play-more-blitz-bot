Notes: what the words mean, how the flashcards work, how your record is saved

Blunders are the PlayMoreBlitz bot's own verdicts, using the same method as Lichess. Good moves: Stockfish looked at every legal move in the position, and a move counts as good if it is almost as strong as the best one. Few good moves means only one or two moves keep the game on track. Game phases use the same rule as Lichess and the bot: the middlegame starts when pieces are developed or traded, the endgame when six or fewer queens, rooks, bishops and knights are left.

Compared with your opponents. Your opponents are the players you met in these same games. For the whole month, each quarter of the map and each game phase, the page compares how often you blundered with how often they did, allowing for how many moves each of you played there. The words go in steps. "More often", "less often" or "about the same" is only said when your games show it clearly. "Probably …" means your games point that way fairly strongly. "Seems …" means they lean that way, but it could still turn out otherwise. "No clear difference yet" means they don't lean either way. "Too few blunders here to compare yet" means there are too few blunders in that part to compare at all. With under 10 games in the month every comparison says "Not enough games to tell yet", and with 10 to 19 games each statement is one step more cautious than the same numbers would get from 20 or more. More games make the picture sharper.

Win chance lost is how much the blunder cut your chance of winning, in points out of 100 (a blunder costs at least 15). The filter buttons apply to the dots, the counts and Next card; the comparison words always use all your blunders. After you answer a card it also says what the blunder did to the game (winning = 70+ out of 100, losing = 30 or less).

Your flashcard record. This page doesn't change: the bot sends a new one when you ask again. Your results are saved by this browser on this device, so they survive a reload or a restart, and next month's page picks them up if you open it in the same browser. They are not sent anywhere. They are lost if you clear your browser's site data or use a private window, and another browser or your phone keeps its own separate record. Your learning settings are kept the same way.

Flashcards. The board shows the position before your blunder, from your side. Play your move on the board: it counts as right if it is one of the good moves. After you answer, the red arrow is the move you played in the game, the green arrow Stockfish's choice and a blue arrow your answer. Previous card steps back through the cards you've opened on this visit. Boxes: every card you try goes into one of five boxes. Get it right when it's due and it moves up a box; get it wrong (or show the answer) and it goes back to box 1; a "Nearly" counts as wrong unless you change that. A box 1 card is due again straight away, box 2 after a day, box 3 after 3 days, box 4 after a week and box 5 after 3 weeks; box 5 counts as learnt. Next card picks due cards first, then ones you haven't tried. Your cards (the link under the flashcard) lists every card with filters, and its Learning settings let you change the waits. Analyse position opens the position on Lichess's analysis board (free, no account needed) so you can try other moves with an engine; the tick or cross on the card still comes from our own deeper count. Chess pieces: the Lichess “cburnett” set by Colin M.L. Burnett (GPLv2+).
